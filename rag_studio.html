See it with a real example

Your customer asks“My package arrived broken. Can I get my money back?”
Your document says“For items damaged in transit, submit a photo within 7 days to request a refund.”

The words are different, but the meaning is connected. RAGForge helps the search connect “arrived broken” with “damaged in transit” — while keeping the photo requirement and 7-day deadline together.

Small changes. Better information.

✂️

Keep the whole rule

A sentence cut in half can lose an important detail. Keep related sentences together so dates, prices, and conditions stay with the facts they explain.

🗣️

Connect everyday words

People say “money back.” Documents say “refund.” Help the search connect common phrases with the words in your documents.

🔎

Find the exact code

One letter can change which product or error you mean. Use exact word matching alongside meaning-based search.

⬆️

Put useful passages first

Some passages answer the question better than others. Take another look and move stronger matches toward the top.

🧹

Clear away repeated text

Three copies of the same fact don't give you three different facts. Reduce repetition to make room for other useful information.

✋

Know when to stop

Sometimes the answer isn't in your documents. A check tries to spot weak evidence and hold back an unsupported answer.

🕵️

Follow the clues behind the answer

Open “See the passages it used” under each answer. It's easier to spot missing details, weak matches, and places where your documents need more explanation.

📦

Take your work with you

Fact cards that make your rules easier to find. Chatbot instructions that tell an assistant how to use them. Settings and sample code for a developer.

🧮

What do the scores mean?

Scores are clues to help you compare results. They aren't promises that every answer is correct. Always check answers against the original text.
